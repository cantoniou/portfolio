Constantinos Antoniou Research Profile
Oct 3, 2026 · @Rakib
At a glance
Prof. Constantinos Antoniou has produced 533 indexed research works since 1997, cited
about 11,800 times, with an h-index of 55 in OpenAlex (53 in Scopus, 69 in Google Scholar).
Two thirds of that output dates from his decade at TUM, and 2025 and 2026 are the two
most productive years of his career.
Beyond the citation counts: 550+ distinct co-authors across 150+ institutions in 40
countries; co-authorship on 24 papers with over 100 citations each; 58% of works open
access; 33 funded projects at TUM since 2017 (10 EU, 6 DFG) with about €3 million TUM
share in active funding; Co-Editor-in-Chief of Transportation Research Part A since 2025;
15+ doctorates completed, 20+ doctoral researchers active, 100+ theses supervised
(anniversary magazine, September 2026). Scopus and Scholar figures come from the
chair's own profile text; OpenAlex figures were extracted on 3 October 2026.
Three decades, three gears
Productivity has stepped up with each career stage rather than drifting: 45 works in the
MIT and industry years (1997 to 2008, about 4 a year), 127 in seven NTUA faculty years
(about 18 a year), and 361 in the TUM decade so far (about 33 a year).

Impact shape
Impact is concentrated in a few landmark papers but rests on a broad base: 407 of 533
works have been cited at least once, 224 ten or more times, and the top 25 papers alone
account for 42% of all citations. The citation record has three peaks, each marking a
different research agenda.

The keyword record tells the same story in finer grain. The most frequent keywords
across the whole career are road safety (57 works), dynamic traffic assignment (42),
travel behavior (35), mode choice (30), microscopic traffic simulation (25) and driver
behavior (24). The method vocabulary of the early years, online calibration (22), extended
Kalman filter (12) and SPSA (12), sits alongside the TUM-era vocabulary of urban air
mobility (21), car sharing (21), shared mobility (19), autonomous vehicles (18) and bike
sharing (18). Two city names recur as study areas: Athens (17 works) and Munich (15).
What has not changed is the quantitative core: statistical and machine-learning methods,
discrete choice and stated-preference surveys (19 works), and simulation are the
common thread from the 2004 PhD on online calibration to the 2020s work on automated
and shared mobility.
Seen through the UN Sustainable Development Goals, 149 works map to SDG 11
(sustainable cities and communities), 43 to SDG 9 (industry, innovation and
infrastructure), 21 to SDG 3 (good health and well-being, mostly road safety) and 13 to
SDG 13 (climate action).

Collaboration network
The network is large, international and visibly multi-generational: 550+ distinct co-
authors, 150+ institutions, 40 countries, and a top co-author list that pairs his own
doctoral advisors with his own doctoral students.
Germany (308 works), Greece (163) and the United States (74) reflect the three career
bases. The next tier is European: United Kingdom 50, Denmark 29, Netherlands 25,
Belgium 23, Switzerland 23, Sweden 20. China (29), Chile (15), Singapore (15), India (13)
and Australia (10) extend the reach beyond Europe. Since 2016 the mix has shifted further
outward: Greece falls to 63 of 362 works while the UK, China, Denmark, the Netherlands
and Belgium all keep or grow their share.

Beyond papers
The publication record sits on top of a comparable record in funding, editing, supervision
and service. The chair's anniversary magazine (September 2026) counts 33 funded
projects at TUM since 2017, twelve of them running: 10 EU projects under H2020 and
Horizon Europe (NOESIS, HumAV, i-DREAMS, MOMENTUM, Drive2theFuture, PHOEBE,
MI-TRAP, AUTOSUP, CulturalRoad, SCUDO), 6 DFG projects including the Collaborative
Research Centre AgiMo (with D-Vanpool, TraMPA, VOTAVI, SEE-FAr, i-TRAUM), 6 German
federal ministry projects (the three MCube projects, PANAMERA and others), 4 TUM
IGSSE projects (MO3, MODA, MINDMAP, ATHLOS), 2 DAAD projects and 5 others
(Bavaria, EIT Urban Mobility, EIG CONCERT-Japan, the Indo-German CRC, TUM IAS).
TUM's share of active funding is about €3.0 million (€1.5 million DFG, €1.2 million Horizon
Europe, €345k IGSSE); the January 2025 profile put third-party funding over the previous
five years at more than €4 million. The largest H2020 consortia had total budgets of €6.3
million (i-DREAMS), €4.0 million (Drive2theFuture) and €2.9 million (MOMENTUM).
